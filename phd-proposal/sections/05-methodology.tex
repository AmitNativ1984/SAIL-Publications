\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Theory}
The theoretical part of this research is based on an extensive literature review in four areas: spiking neural networks; event-based processing and event representations; agile flight; and methods for training control policies.

\subsection{Simulation}
\label{subsec:simulation}
Policies will be trained in GPU-based simulators that run thousands of environments in parallel, such as the Aerial Gym Simulator~\cite{kulkarniAerialGymSimulator2025a} and NVIDIA Isaac Sim. Since the policies will observe events, these simulators will be extended with a simulation of the event camera. Before real flights, the trained policies will be tested with the full flight stack in software-in-the-loop (SITL) and possibly hardware-in-the-loop (HITL) simulation, using simulators such as Gazebo and AirSim.

\subsection{Experiment}
The policies will be tested in real flight. They will be deployed onboard a quadrotor, such as the lab's F450, equipped with an event camera and a neuromorphic processor, and flown through cluttered environments it has not seen before.

\subsection{Analysis}
The policies will be evaluated in simulation and in real flight by their latency, flight performance and flight speed, and compared against benchmark methods.
