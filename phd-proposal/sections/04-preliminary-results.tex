\subsection{Overview}
\label{subsec:prelim_overview}
Fully neuromorphic agile flight requires three parts: event-based sensing, a spiking neural network (SNN) that maps the events to low-level control commands, and neuromorphic hardware that runs it. The first stage of this research isolates the second part, training the SNN controller. We therefore keep the perception conventional: the policy observes a depth image, compressed by a frozen encoder, together with the drone's state, and outputs attitude commands that fly the drone through clutter it has never seen. Replacing the depth image with events is a later stage (Objective~3).

Training a spiking policy for this task raised three problems, each discussed in Section~\ref{sec:related_work} and addressed here:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RL from scratch stalls.} A population-coded spiking actor (PopSAN) trained with PPO from scratch does not progress through the curriculum, while an ANN with the same reward does (Section~\ref{subsec:scratch}). We therefore train an ANN teacher first and distil it into the SNN student, following proximal policy distillation (Section~\ref{subsec:distill}).
    \item \textbf{Distillation is unstable once the teacher is released.} With an adaptive learning rate, the student collapses after the teacher term is annealed out. A constant learning rate keeps it close to the teacher (Section~\ref{subsec:distill}).
    \item \textbf{Encoder neurons can be silent.} A population neuron whose receptive field lies where the observation never goes never fires and carries no information. We set the encoder ranges from the teacher's observation distribution (Section~\ref{subsec:bounds}).
\end{itemize}
The section first defines the problem (Section~\ref{subsec:problem}), then reports the training of each component (Section~\ref{subsec:training_results}), and finally the resulting student: its encoders, spiking activity and estimated energy (Section~\ref{subsec:final_results}).

\subsection{Training Results}
\label{subsec:training_results}
Unless stated otherwise, the success and crash rates below are measured over the training episodes, in which the actions are sampled from the stochastic policy. Deterministic evaluations of the final policies are reported in Section~\ref{subsec:final_results}.

\subsubsection{Deep Collision Encoding}
\label{subsec:vae}
We collected 85,000 depth images in the training environments, with obstacles placed by the task's Poisson process at different densities. The simulated RealSense D435 camera ($320 \times 180$ pixels, $87^\circ$ horizontal field of view, 0.1--10\,m range) was placed at random positions, with roll and pitch up to $\pm 45^\circ$ and uniform yaw, to cover the attitudes seen in flight. Frames with fewer than 5\% valid (in-range) pixels were discarded. For each image, the collision image was precomputed by inflating the obstacles by the drone's radius (Section~\ref{subsec:dce}). Depth $\tilde{d}$ is inverse-normalised as $d = 1 - \tilde{d}/d_{\max}$, with $d_{\max} = 7$\,m, so that near obstacles have large values. A convolutional VAE with a 32-dimensional latent is trained with a reconstruction loss that weights near obstacles more,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} (1+\alpha d_n)(\hat{d}_n-d_n)^2 ,
    \label{eq:recon_loss}
\end{equation}
where $d_n$ is pixel $n$ of the collision image, $\hat{d}_n$ its reconstruction and $\alpha = 5$. The full loss is $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}} + \beta_{\mathrm{KL}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ with $\beta_{\mathrm{KL}} = 0.001$.

A first dataset of 43,000 images was rendered in a stand-alone environment that had drifted from the navigation task: it had no ground plane, lacked some obstacle classes, and used a $1280 \times 720$ camera downsampled offline. Re-collecting the data in the navigation environment itself, with the D435 camera model rendered natively at $320 \times 180$, reduced the validation loss from 0.0175 to 0.0125 ($-28\%$). \autoref{fig:DCE} shows example reconstructions.

\subsubsection{ANN Teacher}
\label{subsec:teacher}
The teacher is a multilayer perceptron actor--critic with separate actor and critic networks (hidden layers 256--256--64, ELU), trained with PPO on 1024 parallel environments. It was trained in two stages (\autoref{fig:teacher_training}). From scratch, with the reward of Section~\ref{subsec:reward}, it climbed the curriculum steadily and reached the top level, 30, after 790 PPO epochs ($1.0 \times 10^8$ environment steps). It was then fine-tuned at level 30 with the measured capture-to-control latency added to the simulation. At level 30 it succeeds in 85.3\% of the training episodes and crashes in 14.2\%. This teacher is the reference the SNN student must match.

\subsubsection{PopSAN Trained from Scratch}
\label{subsec:scratch}
The student is a population-coded spiking actor network (PopSAN)~\cite{tangDeepReinforcementLearning2020} (Section~\ref{sec:related_work}, \autoref{fig:popsan_encoding}). Each of the 49 observation dimensions is encoded by a population of 10 neurons (490 in total), feeding two hidden layers of 256 current-based LIF neurons (synaptic decay $\alpha = 0.5$, membrane decay $\beta = 0.75$, threshold 0.5), simulated for $T = 5$ time steps per control step. Each of the 4 action dimensions is decoded from the firing rates of its own population of 10 output neurons. The critic is an ANN, used only during training.

Trained with PPO from scratch, PopSAN does not progress through the curriculum. \autoref{fig:scratch_curriculum} compares six PopSAN runs with five ANN runs, trained on an earlier version of the task with the same environment and reward for both networks. Four of the five ANN runs reach the top level of that curriculum (25); the best PopSAN run stalls at level 14, and four of the six never leave level 0. Since the ANN learns the task and the SNN does not, the difficulty lies in training the spiking network, not in the task. This motivates training the ANN first and distilling it into the SNN.

\subsubsection{Teacher--Student Distillation}
\label{subsec:distill}
The student is trained with proximal policy distillation~\eqref{eq:ppd_background}~\cite{spiglerProximalPolicyDistillation2025}: it collects its own rollouts and minimises the PPO loss plus the KL divergence between the teacher's and the student's Gaussian action distributions, weighted by $\lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}$. Training runs at curriculum level 30 with the teacher's reward, and the student's critic is initialised from the teacher's critic. The teacher term is annealed out as the student improves. $\lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}$ starts at 0.5 and is reduced by 5\% of its initial value at every curriculum check (every 16 rollouts) in which the student succeeds in at least half of its episodes. Guided by the teacher, the student learns quickly: its success rate reaches 75\% after 100 epochs and 83\% after 200, where PPO from scratch stalled at the lowest levels.

The training was unstable once the teacher was released (\autoref{fig:distill_stability}). With PPO's adaptive learning-rate schedule, which lowers the learning rate when the policy changes too much in an update and raises it otherwise, the student matched the teacher (85\%) while the teacher term was active. Once $\lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}$ reached zero, the schedule raised the learning rate from $\sim 10^{-5}$ to its ceiling of $10^{-3}$, the policy entropy ran away, and the success rate fell to 57\%. With a constant learning rate of $10^{-4}$ the student holds a success rate of 79--84\% after the release, up to epoch 2000, against the teacher's 85\%. Over its last 50 epochs it succeeds in 81.1\% of the episodes and crashes in 18.7\%. The entropy still drifts upwards after the release, more slowly; this growth of the action variance is under investigation.\footnote{The adaptive-learning-rate run used an earlier variant of the field-of-view term, which penalised only horizontal velocity away from the camera axis; the constant-learning-rate run used the teacher's reward.}

\subsubsection{Population Encoder Bounds}
\label{subsec:bounds}
Each encoder neuron $j$ of observation dimension $i$ has a Gaussian receptive field with mean $\mu_{ij}$ and width $\sigma_{ij}$, and receives the constant input current
\begin{equation}
    A_{ij} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(o_i - \mu_{ij})^2}{2\sigma_{ij}^2}\right), \qquad o_i \in [o_i^{\min}, o_i^{\max}],
    \label{eq:grf}
\end{equation}
which drives a non-leaky integrate-and-fire neuron with threshold 0.95 and reset by subtraction for $T = 5$ steps. The neuron fires within the window only if $5A_{ij} \geq 0.95$, i.e.\ $A_{ij} \geq 0.19$. A neuron whose field lies where the observation never goes is therefore silent and carries no information. The observation range $[o_i^{\min}, o_i^{\max}]$ decides where the fields are placed, and observations outside it are clipped.

The ranges are taken from the teacher. The trained teacher flew 200 episodes at curriculum level 30, and for every observation dimension we recorded the 1st and 99th percentiles of its observations, after the same normalisation the policy applies (Section~\ref{subsec:observations}). These percentiles are the bounds $[o_i^{\min}, o_i^{\max}]$. A symmetric range around the mean, as used for input normalisation, misplaces the fields on the many skewed or one-sided dimensions, such as the distance to the goal or several depth latents. Percentiles of the teacher's own flights cover the states the student will visit, since distillation keeps the student close to the teacher.

Within each range, the 10 means are spaced evenly with spacing $\Delta_i = (o_i^{\max} - o_i^{\min})/9$, and the widths are initialised to $\sigma_{ij} = 0.75\,\Delta_i$, so that neighbouring fields overlap. A neuron then fires for any observation within $\sigma_{ij}\sqrt{2\ln(1/0.19)} = 1.37\,\Delta_i$ of its mean, so every value inside the range drives at least two neurons, and every neuron fires for some part of the range the teacher actually visits. Means and widths are learned during training.

\subsubsection{Energy Estimate}
\label{subsec:energy}
We estimate the inference energy per control step from operation counts, as described in Section~\ref{sec:related_work} (Table~\ref{tab:op_energy}). For the ANN teacher's actor (49--256--256--64--4), every connection costs one MAC, \eqref{eq:energy_ann_neuron}: $49 \cdot 256 + 256 \cdot 256 + 256 \cdot 64 + 64 \cdot 4 = 94{,}720$ MACs, or $94{,}720 \times 4.6\,\mathrm{pJ} = 435.7$\,nJ per step. For the SNN student, the synaptic operations of each layer are counted from the measured firing rates of its inputs, \eqref{eq:energy_snn_neuron}, over $T = 5$ time steps. The depth encoder is the same ANN for both policies and is excluded from the comparison.
