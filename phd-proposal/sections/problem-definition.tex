\subsection{Problem Definition}
\label{subsec:problem}

The task is \emph{vision-based navigation through unknown clutter}: a quadrotor must fly from a start position to a goal through an environment it has never seen, using only its onboard sensors, without a prior map and without external knowledge of where the obstacles are. \autoref{fig:policy_overview} shows the policy that solves it: a depth image is compressed into a latent vector, concatenated with the drone's state, and mapped to an attitude command. The ANN teacher, the SNN student and, in later stages of this research, event-based perception are different solutions to this same problem, and are evaluated against the same definition.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{ann_policy_overview.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The navigation policy.} The depth image is compressed by a frozen deep collision encoder (DCE) into a latent $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$, which is concatenated with the drone state $s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{17}$ into the observation $o_t \in \mathbb{R}^{49}$. The policy maps $o_t$ to the attitude command $\mathbf{a}_t \in \mathbb{R}^4$: collective thrust, roll, pitch and yaw rate. Shown here for the ANN teacher; the SNN student replaces the policy block.}
    \label{fig:policy_overview}
\end{figure}

We model the task as a Markov decision process (MDP) $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma)$, where $\mathcal{S}$ is the state of the system, $\mathcal{A}$ the action space of the drone, $\mathcal{P}$ the transition probability, given by the low-level controller and the vehicle dynamics, $\mathcal{R}$ the reward function and $\gamma$ the discount factor. The observations, actions and rewards are defined in the following subsections.

\subsubsection{Observations}
\label{subsec:observations}
The observation concatenates the drone state $s_t$, from onboard state estimation, and the depth latent $z_t$ (\autoref{fig:policy_overview}):
\begin{equation}
    o_t = \big(\underbrace{\mathbf{n}_t,\ \bar{d}_t,\ \mathbf{v}_t,\ \boldsymbol{\omega}_t,\ \mathbf{g}_t,\ \mathbf{a}_{t-1}}_{s_t \,\in\, \mathbb{R}^{17}},\ z_t\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{49},
    \label{eq:drone_state}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathbf{n}_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the unit vector from the drone to the goal, in the vehicle frame (the yaw-only, gravity-aligned heading frame);
    \item $\bar{d}_t \in [0, 1]$ is the distance to the goal, divided by the diagonal of the flight volume;
    \item $\mathbf{v}_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the linear velocity in the vehicle frame;
    \item $\boldsymbol{\omega}_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the body rate measured by the gyroscope, averaged over the control period;
    \item $\mathbf{g}_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the unit gravity direction in the body frame, which encodes roll and pitch;
    \item $\mathbf{a}_{t-1} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ is the previous command \eqref{eq:action} sent to the attitude controller;
    \item $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$ is the latent encoding of the depth image, described below.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Deep collision encoding.}
\label{subsec:dce}
The depth latent $z_t$ is computed by deep collision encoding (DCE)~\cite{kulkarniReinforcementLearningCollisionfree2024,kulkarniTaskdrivenCompressionCollision2023}. The encoder of a variational autoencoder (VAE) compresses the depth image, but its decoder is trained to reconstruct a \emph{collision image} rather than the depth image itself. In the collision image, every obstacle is inflated by the drone's size, so each pixel reports the distance the drone can travel along that ray before it collides (\autoref{fig:DCE}). The latent therefore encodes where the drone can fly rather than what the scene looks like. Since the inflated region depends on both the drone's size and the distance of each point, it cannot be computed with 2D image operations. DCE instead samples points on the edges of the depth image, projects them into 3D, renders a mesh of the drone's volume around each point, and projects these meshes back onto the image plane. The collision image is the pixel-wise minimum of this rendering and the depth image, from which the drone's radius is subtracted.

    \caption{\textbf{Collision-aware depth encoding.} Left: raw $320 \times 180$ depth image from the simulated RealSense D435. Middle: collision image, in which every obstacle is inflated by the drone's collision sphere, so each pixel reports the depth at which the drone's centre would first make contact. Right: reconstruction from the 32-dimensional VAE latent. \subsubsection{Actions}
\label{subsec:actions}
A flight controller is a cascade of controllers: position, velocity, attitude and motor control, each running faster than the one above it. For agile flight the policy should command as low a level as its own control rate allows. Our depth camera runs at 30 frames per second, so we choose attitude commands: low enough to exploit the agility of the vehicle, and high enough that the inner loop runs on the flight controller and absorbs motor-level model errors. The action is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a}_t = \left(f_t,\ \phi_t,\ \theta_t,\ \dot{\psi}_t\right),
    \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
namely the collective thrust $f_t$, from zero to twice the hover thrust, the roll $\phi_t$ and pitch $\theta_t$, bounded to $\pm 45^\circ$, and the yaw rate $\dot{\psi}_t$, bounded to $\pm 60^\circ$/s. The policy outputs each component in $[-1, 1]$, and the command is scaled to these bounds; we write $\bar{\mathbf{a}}_t \in [-1, 1]^4$ for the unscaled action.

\subsubsection{Reward}
\label{subsec:reward}
The reward function gives a terminal reward when the episode ends in success, crash or timeout (Section~\ref{subsec:training_results}) and a sum of five shaping terms at every other step:
\begin{equation}
    r_t =
    \begin{cases}
        10 + 5\,\ell/30 & \text{success}, \\
        -10 & \text{crash}, \\
        -2 & \text{timeout}, \\
        r^{\mathrm{prog}}_t + r^{\mathrm{speed}}_t + r^{\mathrm{jerk}}_t + r^{\mathrm{mag}}_t + r^{\mathrm{fov}}_t & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where $\ell \in \{0, \dots, 30\}$ is the curriculum level, and 30 is the number of curriculum levels, so the success bonus grows from 10 to 15 as the clutter becomes denser. The timeout costs less than a crash, so that waiting is always better than colliding. With $d_t$ the distance to the goal and $v = \lVert \mathbf{v}_t \rVert$ the speed, the shaping terms are
\begin{align}
    r^{\mathrm{prog}}_t &= \lambda_p \left(d_{t-1} - d_t\right), &
    r^{\mathrm{speed}}_t &= -\lambda_v\, v \max\left(0,\ v - v_{\mathrm{max}}\right), \nonumber\\
    r^{\mathrm{jerk}}_t &= -\lambda_j \left\lVert \bar{\mathbf{a}}_t - \bar{\mathbf{a}}_{t-1} \right\rVert, &
    r^{\mathrm{mag}}_t &= \lambda_m \left(e^{-\lVert \bar{\mathbf{a}}_t \rVert^2 / 2} - 1\right), \label{eq:reward_terms}\\
    r^{\mathrm{fov}}_t &= -\lambda_{\mathrm{fov}}\, v^2\, r_{\mathrm{fov}}^2, \nonumber
\end{align}
with $r_{\mathrm{fov}}$ defined in \eqref{eq:rfov} and the weights listed in \autoref{tab:reward}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Parameters of the per-step reward terms \eqref{eq:reward_terms}.}
    \label{tab:reward}
    \begin{tabular}{llr}
        \hline
        Term & Purpose & Parameters \\
        \hline
        Progress $r^{\mathrm{prog}}$ & approach the goal & $\lambda_p = 0.5$ \\
        Speed $r^{\mathrm{speed}}$ & limit excessive speed & $\lambda_v = 0.1$, $v_{\mathrm{max}} = 5$\,m/s \\
        Jerk $r^{\mathrm{jerk}}$ & smooth commands & $\lambda_j = 0.01$ \\
        Action magnitude $r^{\mathrm{mag}}$ & avoid saturated commands & $\lambda_m = 0.04$ \\
        Field of view $r^{\mathrm{fov}}$ & fly where the camera sees & $\lambda_{\mathrm{fov}} = 0.015$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Progress.} $d_t$ is the Euclidean distance from the drone to the goal at step $t$, so the term rewards the distance closed towards the goal in each step.

\paragraph{Speed.} The speed term penalises only speed above $v_{\mathrm{max}}$, so the policy is free to fly fast up to it.

\paragraph{Jerk and action magnitude.} The jerk term penalises changes of the command, and the action-magnitude term penalises its size, so that the policy does not saturate the commands at the action bounds.

\paragraph{Field of view.} The field-of-view term forces the drone to fly only where its camera looks, by penalising velocity directed away from the camera axis. With $\psi_v$ and $\vartheta_v$ the azimuth and elevation of the velocity in the body frame, and $\mathrm{fov}_h$ and $\mathrm{fov}_v$ the horizontal and vertical fields of view of the camera,
\begin{equation}
    r_{\mathrm{fov}} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\psi_v}{\mathrm{fov}_h/2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\vartheta_v}{\mathrm{fov}_v/2}\right)^2},
    \label{eq:rfov}
\end{equation}
which equals 1 on the edge of the field of view.
